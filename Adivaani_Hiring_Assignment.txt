3 Part I – Classical Neural Machine Translation
3.1 Objective
In this section, you are required to design and implement a complete Neural Machine Translation
(NMT) framework for Hindi↔Marathi translation using recurrent neural architectures.
3.2 T ask Description
You must build a Seq2Seq translation framework consisting of an LSTM encoder, an LSTM
decoder, with aAttentionmechanism. Beyond these requirements, you are free to make archi-
tectural and engineering decisions regarding the decoding strategy, beam search methodology,
optimization pipeline, tokenization strategy, curriculum learning setup, or training dynamics.
A major objective of this section is to evaluate how classical sequence modeling architectures
behave under different representation learning settings.
You are required to perform experiments under at least the following embedding configurations:
1. Randomly initialized embeddings on both source and target sides.
2. BERT-based embeddings on both source and target sides using the following pretrained
models:
Hindi BERT:
https://huggingface.co/l3cube-pune/hindi-bert-v2
Marathi BERT:
https://huggingface.co/l3cube-pune/marathi-bert-v2
You are encouraged to explore additional ideas such as shared embeddings, SentencePiece/BPE
tokenization, subword vocabulary sharing, frozen versus trainable embeddings, cross-lingual
embedding alignment, scheduled sampling, label smoothing, or beam search decoding strategies
if you believe they improve the system.
An important component of this section is the comparative analysis between randomly initial-
ized embeddings and pretrained BERT-based embeddings. Your experiments and report must
critically analyze how the two embedding strategies affect convergence behavior, optimization
stability, semantic preservation, translation fluency, low-frequency word handling, and final
translation quality.
3.3 Evaluation Requirements
Your experiments must include BLEU (100) and CHRF++ (100) evaluations, where the scores
are reported on a scale from 0 to 100. In addition, you must provide train and validation loss
curves, train and validation BLEU (100) plots, and train and validation CHRF++ (100) plots.
Your report must contain a comparative quantitative discussion analyzing the differences be-
tween random embeddings and BERT-based embeddings across BLEU (100) and CHRF++
(100) scores. The discussion should clearly explain observed performance differences, strengths,
limitations, convergence characteristics, and linguistic behavior under both embedding settings.
Your report should also contain qualitative analysis involving example translations and com-
parative observations between the different embedding configurations explored during experi-
mentation.

4 Part II – Language Model Pretraining and Translation
4.1 Objective
The second section of the assessment focuses on pretraining language models from scratch and
exploring how these pretrained representations can be utilized for machine translation.
This section is intentionally designed to be research-oriented and open-ended. We are partic-
ularly interested in your reasoning process, creativity, architectural understanding, and your
ability to connect ideas from modern language modeling literature to practical machine trans-
lation systems.
4.2 Pretraining Requirements
You are required to pretrain the following models entirely from scratch:
1. A BERT-like encoder model containing approximately 110M parameters. The NSP (Next
Sentence Prediction) objective must be removed, and you are expected to design your own
masked language modeling pipeline.
2. A GPT-2 style decoder-only model containing approximately 124M parameters.
Your Transformer implementations must incorporate the following architectural modifications:
•Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) instead of sinusoidal positional embeddings,
•Grouped Query Attention (GQA) instead of standard multi-head attention,
•RMSNorm instead of LayerNorm.
4.3 Core Challenge
After pretraining these models, you must use them for machine translation.
How you choose to accomplish this is intentionally left open. You may pretrain more than one
time or use any other principled methodology that you believe is technically sound.
The objective here is not simply reproducing an existing paper, but demonstrating your ability
to reason about how pretrained language representations can be leveraged for translation tasks
under practical constraints.

4.4 Hint
A BERT-style model fundamentally learns bidirectional contextual representations,
whereas GPT-style models specialize in autoregressive generation.
Think carefully about how these complementary properties might be jointly exploited
inside a translation framework.
You may also study existing architectural foundations such as:
google-bert/bert-base-cased
openai-community/gpt2
to better understand architectural philosophy, training dynamics, autoregressive decoding
behavior, contextual representation learning, and modern language modeling principles.
You are encouraged to reason carefully about how encoder-style and decoder-style pre-
trained representations differ, and how these differences may influence downstream trans-
lation quality, generation stability, and linguistic generalization.
4.5 Expected Discussion Areas
Your report should ideally discuss why your chosen architecture should work, what trade-offs are
involved, the scaling limitations encountered during experimentation, optimization challenges,
training stability issues, parameter efficiency considerations, failure cases, and possible future
improvements.
We are particularly interested in whether you can critically analyze your own approach instead
of merely presenting successful results.
5 Evaluation Philosophy
This assessment is deliberately research-oriented.
We are not only evaluating whether your models perform well, but whether you understand what
you are building, whether you can reason rigorously about architectural decisions, whether you
can engineer reproducible systems, and whether you can communicate technical ideas with
clarity and precision.
